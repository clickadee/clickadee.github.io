Clickadee is fully remote, with all sessions held on Zoom.

September 26 – December 5, 2026

  • Studio sessions (10 total): Every Saturday, September 26–November 28, 1–3 PM EST
  • Lab meetings (8 total): Every Wednesday, September 30–November 18, 7–8 PM EST
  • 1:1s (2 total): Scheduled individually with each student
  • Demo Day: Saturday, December 5, 1–3 PM EST

Studio sessions are live lectures introducing new concepts, with group and individual coding exercises woven throughout.

Lab meetings are research-focused. They will be a rotating mix of project office hours, guest speakers, research paper discussions, and stand-up-style updates from students.

Demo Day is when students present their research projects! Family and friends are welcome to join and celebrate.

Clickadee Cohort 1.0 is designed for students who've been introduced to Python. Students should be comfortable using variables and loops, defining and calling functions, and writing print statements.

No Python experience yet? This free Kaggle course (about 5 hours) will get your student up to speed.

Grades 9–12.

About 6 hours a week: a 2-hour studio session, a 1-hour lab meeting, and roughly 3 hours for independent coding exercises and project work.

A computer with a stable internet connection, a working camera and microphone, and support for Zoom. All programming happens in Google Colab, so students will need a Google account.

Reach out to me ahead of time if your student can't make it. Every session is recorded, so students can catch up and parents can follow along.

Each student identifies their own research question, finds a public dataset, and designs their own machine learning method to build a research project that is uniquely their own.

Students document their process weekly in the Clickadee Lab Notebook and get feedback directly in it. Notebooks are shared with parents, so you can follow along too!

By the end, each student writes a research blog post (published on the Clickadee website) and presents their project at Demo Day.

Because this isn't a cookie-cutter project, results will vary. Some students' hypotheses won't hold up, and that's a normal, valuable part of doing real research!

I select students based on the interest they share in the short application form. Keep in mind that extensive programming or machine learning experience is not required for Cohort 1.0. I am looking for beginners!

  • 28 hours of live group instruction
  • Personalized feedback on all code your student writes
  • Two 1:1 mentoring sessions with me
  • Learning from guest speakers about real research happening at Harvard
  • A recording of every session
  • A cohort capped at 15 students

Cohort 1.0, as the first cohort, will be priced at a discount: $3,000.

I'm currently designing a curriculum for 1–2 follow-up courses that will build on this first course into more advanced topics, running in 2027. All Cohort 1.0 students get priority acceptance.

Beyond that, I'm happy to keep mentoring your student directly, or connect them with someone in my Stanford/Harvard network for continued 1:1 work.
